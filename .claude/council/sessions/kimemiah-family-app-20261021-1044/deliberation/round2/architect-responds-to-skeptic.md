## Architect Response to Skeptic

**Their position:** Keep the PIN as a server-checked, rate-limited gate. After the PIN, the person picks their name, which signs them in as one of 6 real Supabase Auth users. Offline writes only where signal fails. Append-only money and stock records with an audit log, nightly off-site `pg_dump` backups, and a core-first build order.

**My response:**
1. **Offline scope: Modify.** I still copy every table into Dexie, but only as a read cache, so all 8 sections can be read with no signal. Offline writes are limited to these: stock movements, shopping ticks, "Put away", shopping extras and money entries. Every other write needs a connection, and the button says so. The outbox stays generic; it just accepts fewer mutation types.
2. **Auth: Modify, and adopt theirs.** Six member users mean `auth.uid()` is the member. RLS and the audit trail then come from the session itself, not from an `actor_id` the client sends. That's simpler than anonymous users per device plus a claim plus a `devices` table. I drop `devices` and `unlock_attempts` (rate limiting moves to a small `auth_failures` table), and add a client-generated `device_id` column to the audit log. "Switch person" is a server route that needs a valid household session. Revoking access becomes "change PIN + sign out all", which is enough for 6 people.
3. **Backups: Maintain theirs, and defer the details to Operator.** Nightly `pg_dump` from a GitHub Action, encrypted with age before upload, plus one restore drill. The database URL goes in GitHub secrets. Caveat: I'm not certain a direct Postgres connection counts as "activity" for the free-tier pause. The same Action should also send one REST call to be safe.
4. **Money: Maintain, converged.** `money_entries` is insert-only. `reverses_id` is renamed `voids_entry_id`, and `recorded_by` (the session) is kept separate from `spent_by`. A trigger writes `audit_log` rows for every table.

**Reasoning:** Skeptic is right that I designed the sync layer around the hardest possible case when the real conflicts sit in four places. Narrowing offline writes removes whole classes of bugs: offline job edits, offline meeting notes, offline budget edits. The core of my design is unchanged and Skeptic adopted it independently: stock as deltas with idempotent client UUIDs, soft deletes, and pulls by `updated_at` cursor. On auth, their version gives the same per-person attribution with fewer moving parts and a real `auth.uid()`, so I concede. One thing I won't give up: job and cook-slot claims are still first-wins and checked on the server, even if offline claims are dropped. Without that, two people think they both have the market run.
